# Journal guidelines (checked 22 November 2026)

Requirements of the journals most likely for the thesis papers, read from each
journal's own author page on the date above. Limits change: re-read the page before
submitting, and update this file. Numbers are as stated by the journals; the
wording is paraphrased.

## Nature (Articles)

Source: <https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/formatting-guide>, the annotated
summary paragraph (<https://www.nature.com/documents/nature-summary-paragraph.pdf>,
version of September 2019), and <https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/for-authors/write>.

- **Summary paragraph.** It is fully referenced, ideally ≤ 200 words and separate from
  the main text. It avoids numbers, abbreviations, acronyms and measurements unless
  they are essential, and is written for readers outside the discipline. The
  annotated example builds it in seven parts:
  1. 1–2 sentences of basic introduction, understandable to a scientist in any field.
  2. 2–3 sentences of more detailed background, for scientists in related fields.
  3. 1 sentence stating the general problem the study addresses.
  4. 1 sentence with the main result ("Here we show" or equivalent).
  5. 2–3 sentences on what the result reveals compared with earlier thinking, or what
     it adds.
  6. 1–2 sentences placing the results in a general context.
  7. Optionally, 2–3 sentences of broader perspective, for any scientist; the
     paragraph may then reach ~300 words.
- **Length.**
  - A 6-page article has ~2,500 words and 4 modest display items; an 8-page article
    has ~4,300 words and 5–6 display items.
  - A modest display item with its legend takes about a quarter page (~270 words). A
    composite figure needing half a page costs about 600 words.
- **Subheadings:** up to 40 characters.
- **Readability.**
  - Write clearly and simply for readers in other disciplines and for readers whose
    first language is not English.
  - Avoid jargon. Explain unavoidable terms concisely but not didactically, and keep
    abbreviations to a minimum.
  - Editors often rewrite titles and summary paragraphs so that the conclusions are
    clear to a broad readership.
  - Spelling follows the Oxford English Dictionary.
- **Methods.**
  - Concise, but enough to interpret and replicate the results; typically ≤ 3,000
    words, with short bold subheadings.
  - Cite methods that are already published, and state only what was added or changed.
  - No figures in Methods. Separate Data Availability and Code Availability statements.
- **Figures and legends.**
  - Each legend begins with a brief title for the whole figure, then describes each
    panel and symbol, without method details. Under 300 words. Every error bar and
    statistic is defined.
  - Figures are as small and simple as clarity allows, and understandable to readers
    in related fields. Data that fit in a short sentence go in the text.
  - Units have a space before them (SI), thousands take commas, and keys go in the
    legend where possible.
  - Nature encourages a simple schematic summarising the main finding (in Extended Data).
- **Writing advice** ("How to write your paper").
  - State the main finding so that the title and abstract show what was found and why
    it matters.
  - Explain the research question early, without assuming specialist background.
  - Use technical terms only where needed; define abbreviations and use them sparingly.
  - Prefer the active voice ("we performed").
  - Make each figure earn its place.
  - Put technical support that the narrative does not need into the SI.
  - Ask a colleague outside your field to read the title and abstract.
- **Acknowledgements:** brief; no effusive adjectives ("excellent assistance").
- **Reporting Summary:** required for life-science articles.

## Nature Communications (Article)

Source: <https://www.nature.com/ncomms/submit/article>.

- The format is flexible at first submission. As a guide:
  - main text ≤ 5,000 words (excluding abstract, Methods, references and legends);
  - title ≤ 15 words;
  - abstract ≤ 200 words with no references. It serves as a general introduction and a
    brief, non-technical summary of the results and their implications.
- **Sections:**
  - a headed Introduction, then Results, then Discussion and Methods;
  - Results and Methods have topical subheadings;
  - the Discussion is succinct, without subheadings;
  - Methods are typically < 3,000 words.
- **Display items and references:** legends ≤ 350 words; up to 10 display items (≤ 4
  when the text is under 2,000 words); about 70 references at most.

## PNAS (Research Reports)

Source: <https://www.pnas.org/author-center/submitting-your-manuscript>.

- **Length.** Preferred 6 pages: ~4,000 words, 50 references and 4 medium display items;
  maximum 12 pages. A Brief Report is 3 pages (~1,600 words, 15 references).
- **Abstract:** ≤ 250 words explaining the major contributions to the general reader.
- **Significance statement:** ≤ 120 words, written so that an undergraduate-educated
  scientist outside the field understands why the research matters.
- **Title.** < 135 characters, descriptive, for a broad audience. It names the studied
  organism and has no numbers, acronyms, abbreviations, punctuation or puns. At least
  3 keywords.
- **Main text:**
  - no statements of novelty or priority;
  - no laboratory jargon;
  - SI units;
  - field-specific abbreviations defined at first mention;
  - CSE Manual style.
- **Supporting information.**
  - The main text must stand on its own. Methods moved to the SI must still be
    described well enough in the main text to follow the logic.
  - A paper about a new method describes it fully in the main text.
- **Figure legends:**
  - first sentence an overview of the whole figure;
  - each panel described;
  - error bars explained;
  - whether ± means SEM or SD;
  - the number of data points (N);
  - numerical axes start at 0 (except log axes).
- **Data:** a data sharing plan for all data, documentation and code. Archived code is
  cited (for example on Zenodo).

## Global Ecology and Biogeography (Research Article)

Source: <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14668238/homepage/forauthors.html>.

- **Scope test.** Broad-scale, general patterns and processes, with conclusions that
  matter to ecologists and biogeographers everywhere, not only to one place or taxon.
  - Many papers are declined without review, judged on scope and on whether the
    Abstract and display items show a conceptual advance of global relevance.
  - Put the most citable points in the Abstract and the figures.
  - The cover letter needs a paragraph of < 250 words on why the paper interests GEB
    readers.
- **Length:** ~10 printed pages (~5,000 words, 50 references, 6–8 display items).
  Longer papers need a justification.
- **Structured abstracts (≤ 300 words).**
  - Research Article: Aim, Location, Time period, Major taxa studied, Methods,
    Results, Main conclusions.
  - Method paper: Aim, Innovation, Main conclusions.
  - Data Article (~2,000 words): Motivation, Main types of variable contained, Spatial
    location and grain, Time period and grain, Major taxa and level of measurement,
    Software format.
- **Title, running title and keywords.**
  - Title short and informative, with the main keywords and no abbreviations.
  - Running title < 40 characters.
  - 6–10 keywords in alphabetical order; the most important ones repeated in the title.
- **Language and review:** British spelling; double-anonymous review.
- **Data and code.**
  - Both must be accessible to reviewers and archived in a stable public repository,
    with links in the Data and Code Availability statement and the data cited.
  - GitHub and private websites do not count as stable. Licences CC0 or CC-BY.
  - Follow the FAIR and CARE principles.
- **Tables and legends** stand alone: they name the study region and taxon, give n,
  units and abbreviations.

## Journal of Biogeography (Research Article)

Source: <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/13652699/homepage/forauthors.html>.

- **Scope.** Articulate the theoretical foundations and the conceptual advance. Papers
  should be original, concise, rigorously argued and consequential. Exceptional case
  studies are welcome when they shift preconceptions.
- **Research Article.** ≤ 6,000 words; about 6 tables and figures. Main headers:
  Introduction, Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion. A Letter is ≤ 4,000 words
  with an abstract ≤ 250 words.
- **Structured abstract (≤ 300 words)**, written as factual statements:
  - **Aim:** the principal question or hypotheses.
  - **Location.**
  - **Taxon:** the main group studied.
  - **Methods:** materials, sampling and analysis.
  - **Results.**
  - **Main conclusions:** the take-home message.
- **Methods** must let a competent practitioner repeat the study, or the code must be
  provided.
- **Data papers** need:
  - the scope;
  - protocols;
  - every data field and its metadata;
  - the data records;
  - a technical validation;
  - usage notes with caveats.
  
  This is the same structure as krapp2021.
- **Title, keywords, spelling and review.**
  - Title ≤ 115 characters, with no abbreviations or Latin binomials.
  - Running title < 40 characters; 6–10 keywords.
  - American or British spelling, never mixed. Double-anonymous review.

## Molecular Ecology (Original Article)

Source: <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/1365294x/homepage/forauthors.html>.

- **Length and abstract:** ≤ 8,000 words from Abstract to Discussion; abstract about
  250 words, containing the major keywords; 4–6 keywords.
- **Order:** Title page, Abstract, Introduction, Materials and Methods, Results
  (separate from the Discussion), Discussion, Acknowledgements, References, Data
  Accessibility and Benefit-Sharing, Author Contributions, Tables and Figures.
- **Title.** The main keywords within the first 65 characters, and no abbreviations.
  For a catchy title, the journal suggests the form "Accurate title with keywords:
  catchy part".
- **Data.**
  - All data, metadata and code go to reviewers at submission, and are archived at
    acceptance.
  - Software needs a snapshot on Zenodo, CRAN or Dryad besides GitHub.
  - The Data Accessibility Statement names where each data type is (raw reads in SRA
    or ENA, genotypes on Dryad, metadata with coordinates and dates).
  - A Benefit-Sharing Statement under the Nagoya Protocol is encouraged; compliance
    with national laws is required. This is relevant for African samples.

## What the journals agree on

- **Title and abstract readable outside the field**, stating the main finding (Nature,
  Nature Communications, PNAS); the keywords at the front (GEB, J Biogeogr, Mol Ecol).
- **Aim as a question or hypothesis** and a take-home "Main conclusions": the
  structured abstracts of the biogeography journals force both.
- **Legends that stand alone** and define every statistic, symbol and n.
- **Methods detailed enough to repeat the study**: cite published methods, give code.
- **Data and code in a stable archive with a DOI**, available to reviewers. A
  repository on GitHub alone is not enough.
- **No novelty or priority claims, no jargon, few abbreviations.**
